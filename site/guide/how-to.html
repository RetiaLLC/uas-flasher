Calibrate a place

Calibrate each time you arrive at a new place, before you rely on the unit there. A calibration learns the signals that belong to the place so that they don't raise alerts.

To calibrate a place, follow these steps:

  1. Switch off every aircraft, remote control, video transmitter, and pair of goggles that you want the unit to alert for later. If they're on the air during the calibration, the unit learns their video transmitter, their control link, and the Wi-Fi network of an aircraft that's flown from a phone.

  2. On the master, press PORTS and check that both ports show UP. A sensor that isn't UP learns nothing.

  3. Press CAL.

  4. If the unit was last used at another place, press FORGET ALL. A calibration only adds signals to the list.

  5. Press 60 S or 5 MIN. The unit learns only the signals that appear while the calibration runs, so a longer run catches signals that appear only now and then. Don't use 30 S for most places. At one place with two fixed cameras, a 30-second run learned only one of them, and the other one raised two alerts afterward.

    The screen shows a countdown. To end the calibration early and keep what it learned, press DONE. To end it and discard what it learned, press CANCEL.

  6. Read the result on the Cal screen.

The Cal screen shows Meaning What to do
LAST RUN: 3 NEW, 1 STRONGER The unit learned three signals. It also heard one known signal at a higher level, and now knows that signal up to the new level. Read the lines under it.
A line such as VID F8 5880 MHz or SUB 866.4 MHz One known signal, with its tag, name, and frequency. Decide whether the signal could be your own equipment. If it is, forget it, switch the equipment off, and calibrate again.
1 AIRCRAFT SIGNAL: NOT LEARNED An aircraft broadcast Remote ID or DJI's own identity element during the run. The unit never learns these signals, and it alerted for this one during the run. If the aircraft is yours, it was on when you thought it was off. Otherwise, do nothing.
NO ROOM FOR 2 MORE: FORGET SOME The list is full at 32 signals. Press FORGET ALL and calibrate again.
PORT B NOT UP: NOT LEARNED FROM That sensor was missing for part of the run. Get the port to UP and calibrate again.
NO ALERT FOR 4 MIN 12 S How long the unit has gone without raising an alert. Watch the time grow for a few minutes.

If the unit still alerts after the calibration, identify the signal first. Recalibrate only for a signal that you've established belongs to the place's background. An unexplained alert might be a signal that you want to detect.

CLEAR after a calibration means that the unit reports no signal that it hasn't learned. It doesn't mean that nothing is in the air.

A fixed wireless camera on a video channel sends a real video signal. The unit is right to alert for it, because the radio signal of a fixed camera looks the same as an aircraft's video. Calibration is the way to handle a fixed camera.

A known signal has a cost. On its frequency, or under its name, the unit alerts only for a signal that's more than 10 dB stronger than the learned level. A known video channel makes the unit less sensitive on that channel. A learned control link makes the unit less sensitive to every control link that uses the same packet rate. Calibration can therefore quiet a later transmitter on the same carrier or packet rate.

Forget a known signal

To forget every known signal, on the master press CAL, and then press FORGET ALL.

The screen has no button that forgets a single signal. To forget one signal, use the USB connection and the signal's key. The commands use tools/north.py, which isn't publicly available. To use the commands, you need access to the project's repository.

  1. List the known signals and find the key of the one to forget:

    python3 tools/north.py --port PORT get what=known

  2. Forget the signal by its key:

    python3 tools/north.py --port PORT calibrate 'forget=va|5880'

For what PORT is, see Change a setting over USB. The keys are listed in the alert logic's list of item keys.

Choose a sensitivity

Each of the three kinds of sensor has three levels: low, medium, and high. A new unit is set to medium.

You can't change the sensitivity on the unit's screen. To change it, use one of the following:

  • The app, for all sensors at once or one at a time: the RF sensor, the Remote ID sensor, and the 868 MHz watch. See the Android app's notes.
  • A USB connection, with the settings sens_rf, sens_rid, and sens_sg. See Change a setting over USB.

Reduce false alarms

Try the following in order. Each step costs less range than the next one.

  1. Calibrate the place. Calibration quiets the place's own signals and nothing else.
  2. Mute the one signal for a set time.
  3. Set that kind of sensor to low.

The following table shows what low changes, compared with medium.

Sensor At low
RF sensor A signal must be 10 dB above the noise, compared with 8 dB at medium. This costs about 4 dB of range. A control link must last 15 s, and a video carrier 3 s, before it's more than a notice.
Remote ID sensor An ID, a Wi-Fi link, or an aircraft's network that the sensor hears only below -85 dBm is a notice. The unit doesn't list other Wi-Fi names.
868 MHz watch A signal must be heard in four different seconds out of eight, compared with three at medium. Control must last 10 s, compared with 8 s at medium.

Increase range

High changes less than its name suggests:

  • For video, a threshold of 6 dB detected nothing more than 8 dB did.
  • On 868 MHz, high is medium without the 8 s that control must last.
  • High also lists every device that asks for a drone's network.

Most measurements of the unit were made at medium. Each level was measured in one run only.

Mute a signal

To mute a signal, follow these steps:

  1. On the Home screen, tap the signal's row.

  2. Do one of the following:

    • To mute this one signal, press 15 MIN or 1 HOUR.
    • To mute every signal of its kind for an hour, press the button that names the kind, such as ALL VIDEO or ALL SUB-GHZ.

    The row turns faint, its tag changes to MUTE, and the minutes left appear where the signal bars were.

To remove a mute, tap the row, and then press UNMUTE. This removes every mute that covers the signal: its own mute and a mute of its whole kind.

A mute ends automatically when its time is up. When the master restarts, it forgets every mute.

A mute of a whole kind stops the unit from detecting that kind for the hour, including an aircraft that arrives later. Mute the one signal when you can.

For a mute longer than an hour, use the mute command over USB. See the northbound stream in the master's README.

Follow one signal

Follow a signal to find out whether it gets stronger or weaker as you move.

To follow a signal, follow these steps:

  1. Tap the signal's row. FOLLOW is lit if the signal can be followed. The unit can follow a video carrier or a verified control link on 2.4 GHz, if the unit's RF sensor was built with the seeker. See the RF sensor's user guide.
  2. Press FOLLOW. The Seeker screen opens.
  3. Move, and watch the arrow and the number. The number is the change in signal strength, in decibels, since you pressed FOLLOW. The beeps get faster as the signal gets stronger.
  4. When you're done, press STOP. A seek that you started on the screen ends automatically after 300 s.

While you follow one signal, the RF sensor watches only that signal. It doesn't notice new control or video links until the seek ends. Remote ID and the 868 MHz watch continue.

For details, see seeker mode in the master's README.

Pair a phone

The app isn't publicly available.

To pair a phone, follow these steps:

  1. On the master, press PHONE and note the name, such as UAS Master-1A2B.
  2. In the app, find the master and select that name.
  3. When the master shows PAIRING REQUEST, press ALLOW. The request waits 20 s.
  4. On the phone, type the six digits that the master shows, within about 30 seconds.

Optional: Press PAIR A PHONE before you start. The phone then finds the master sooner. PAIR A PHONE opens pairing for 120 s and disconnects any phone that's connected.

Problem What to do
The code expired before you typed it. In the app, select the master again. After you press ALLOW, pairing stays open for 120 s, so you don't need to press it again.
The master shows NO ROOM LEFT: FORGET PHONES FIRST. The master remembers three phones, which is the most it can hold. Forget all phones, and then pair again.
The phone was paired, has forgotten the master, and now fails to connect. On the master, press PAIR A PHONE. Then, in the app, select the master.
The master has forgotten the phone, and the phone still tries to connect. In the phone's Bluetooth settings, remove the master. Then pair again.
The pairing request doesn't appear. During an alert at DETECT or higher, the screen doesn't change on its own. Press PHONE.

For the rules behind this table, see the phone link in the master's README.

Forget all phones

On the master, press PHONE, and then press FORGET PHONES. The master forgets every phone it remembers, and the connection ends.

Record a walk

A walk shows how a signal's level falls as you carry the unit away from its transmitter, until the unit lists nothing.

The commands in this section use tools/north.py and tools/walk_report.py, which aren't publicly available. To use the commands, you need access to the project's repository.

Before the walk

  1. Insert a card, and then switch the unit on. The Ports screen shows a line such as CARD: 3 LINES in green.

  2. Connect a phone once. This sets the master's clock, so the log lines carry the time of day.

  3. Turn on the walk log over USB:

    python3 tools/north.py --port PORT set 'cfg={"walk_log":true}'

    The Ports screen now shows WALK LOG where it showed CARD. The setting stays on after a restart.

During the walk

  1. Leave the transmitter where it is, and leave it switched on.
  2. Carry the unit away, and then back.
  3. Write down the time of each thing you do, such as when the transmitter came on and where you turned. Use the clock that the master shows.

After the walk

  1. Turn off the walk log with the same command, using false in place of true. While the walk log is on and the master gets positions, the card records where the unit has been.

  2. Switch the master off, and then remove the card.

  3. On a computer, run the report:

    python3 tools/walk_report.py UAS00012.LOG
python3 tools/walk_report.py --home last --step 10 --csv walk.csv UAS00012.LOG

    The report is a table of time, distance, and level. It shows what the master listed, and what the sensors still tracked while the master listed nothing. It doesn't print positions. For its options, see the tools' notes on reading a walk.

The report takes distances from the positions that a phone gave the master during the walk. The position mark in the top bar is lit while the master has a position. Without positions, the report gives levels over time and no distances. The distances are the distances you walked, between places that the phone measured. The unit doesn't work out a distance from a signal.

If a write to the card fails, the log can have a gap of a minute. The master unmounts the card and starts a new file a minute later.

Read the log on the card

  1. Switch the master off before you remove the card. Removing a card from a running master hasn't been tested.
  2. On a computer, open the folder UASLOG. The files are named UAS00001.LOG and up, with a new file each time the master starts. The highest number is the newest.
  3. Read the lines. Each line is one record as text:
    • The first line of a file shows which firmware and settings wrote the file.
    • An alert line holds the level and every signal in the list at that moment.
    • Lines carry time after the clock is set.
  4. Optional: For a table in place of the lines, run tools/walk_report.py on the file, as in Record a walk.

If the master was given positions, the card holds places and times: in every alert, and in every position line while the walk log is on. Protect a card as you protect a phone.

The master never deletes anything. Remove old logs on a computer. When less than 8 MB of space is left, the master treats the card as full and stops writing.

For what each kind of line holds, see the card in the master's README.

Change a setting over USB

Use a USB connection for the settings that the unit's screen can't change: volume, brightness, region, walk_log, and the sensitivity levels if you don't have a phone.

The commands use tools/north.py, which isn't publicly available. To use the commands, you need access to the project's repository.

You need the following:

  • A computer with Python 3 and pyserial.
  • The project's tools.
  • A USB cable to the master's own port.

PORT is the master's serial device, such as /dev/cu.usbmodem1101 on a Mac or /dev/ttyACM0 on Linux. The name changes with the USB port that the cable is in.

python3 tools/north.py --port PORT get what=settings
python3 tools/north.py --port PORT set 'cfg={"volume":40}'
python3 tools/north.py --port PORT set 'cfg={"brightness":70}'
python3 tools/north.py --port PORT set 'cfg={"sens_rf":"low"}'

The first command prints the current settings. The other commands print the master's reply, which ends in "ok":true when the master accepted the setting.

Write the part in braces without spaces.

The master keeps settings in its flash memory when it restarts.

For every setting and its limits, see sensitivity in the master's README.

Opening the port might restart the master on some computers. A restart forgets every mute and starts a new log file.

Update the master's firmware

To update the master from a browser, follow these steps:

  1. Connect a USB-C cable that carries data to the master's own USB-C port.
  2. On a computer, open the firmware page in Chrome or Edge.
  3. Close any other program that uses the master's port, such as a serial monitor.
  4. Choose a release:
    • Alpha has master firmware 0.11.0 with the portrait screen, and Remote ID sensor firmware 0.7.3. The page offers it first.
    • Beta has master firmware 0.12.0 with the landscape screen, and nothing for the sensors. It might contain bugs.
  5. Click FLASH THE MASTER, and then select the board in the browser's list. The page shows each step and ends with DONE.
  6. On the master, press PORTS. The version is at the bottom of the screen, such as MASTER 0.12.0.
  7. Check that the master kept its known signals on the Cal screen, its settings, and your phone's pairing.

An update keeps the master's settings, paired phones, and learned signals. The first flash of a new board erases its settings area. To erase them during an update, select the checkbox on the master's card before you click FLASH THE MASTER. A firmware that can't read the stored settings starts from the defaults.

Advanced: build from source

If you have access to the project's repository, you can build and flash the firmware yourself.

  1. Install ESP-IDF 5.5.1, which is the version the firmware was built with.
  2. Build and flash as the master's build instructions describe.

Flash the normal build. A bench build is for tests. It writes pairing codes to its log, so anyone who reads its USB port can pair a phone with it. See bench builds in the master's README.

Update a sensor's firmware

Each sensor is flashed through its own USB-C port. Released master firmware can't update a sensor.

  1. Keep the battery connected, or the master's USB cable, while you flash. A cable in the sensor alone doesn't supply enough power for the master and the other sensor. See flashing a XIAO in the assembled unit.
  2. Flash the sensor:
    • For the Remote ID sensor, the firmware page can flash it through its own USB-C port. This hasn't been tested on a real sensor.
    • The firmware page has no RF sensor firmware.
    • If you have access to the project's repository, build and flash as the sensor's build instructions describe: Remote ID sensor or RF sensor. Both warn against letting the flashing tool reset the chip in the usual way. Read that part before your first flash.
  3. On the master, press PORTS. The sensor's port shows UP, and FIRMWARE under it shows the new version.

Each sensor's user guide has the steps: Remote ID sensor and RF sensor.

Prepare for a demonstration or a test day

This checklist covers what the firmware does. It doesn't make the unit detect what it can't detect. Before the day, compare the aircraft you expect with what the unit can detect.

The day before

  • ☐ Charge the battery with a USB supply of 2 A or more in the master's own port. While it charges, the battery icon shows full whatever the charge is. To read the charge, unplug the USB cable.
  • ☐ Bring a USB supply for a long day. How long a full battery runs the unit hasn't been measured.
  • ☐ Fit all three antennas, and tighten them.
  • ☐ Check that the card is FAT32, has space, and held a log the last time. The Ports screen shows CARD: … LINES in green.
  • ☐ Press PORTS and write down the three versions: the master's at the bottom, and FIRMWARE under each port. Record them with every result.
  • ☐ Pair the phone, and check that the phone's clock is correct. The master takes its time from the phone.
  • ☐ Set the sensitivity levels and the volume. A new unit has medium and a volume of 60.

At the site, before anything flies

  • ☐ Put the unit where its antennas are as high and as unobstructed as possible, such as on a pole or a stand, not on the ground or in a bag. Raising the antennas is the easiest way to extend the range for a transmitter near the ground.
  • ☐ Ask for every aircraft, remote control, video transmitter, and pair of goggles of the day to be switched off.
  • ☐ Switch the unit on, and check that it shows SCANNING with a green dot.
  • ☐ Press PORTS, and check that each sensor shows UP, RADIO RUNNING, and SETTINGS OK, and that RECORDS LOST isn't climbing.
  • ☐ Connect the phone once, and check that the top bar shows the time.
  • ☐ Press CAL, and then press FORGET ALL. What the unit learned at another place doesn't belong here.
  • ☐ Press 60 S, or 5 MIN if the place is busy. Don't use 30 S.
  • ☐ Read what the unit learned, line by line. From now on, each line stays quiet unless it returns more than 10 dB stronger. A fixed wireless camera on a video channel is the usual finding.
  • ☐ If a line is one of the day's own aircraft or remote controls, forget it, switch the equipment off, and calibrate again.
  • ☐ Watch NO ALERT FOR on the Cal screen for a few minutes. Then press HOME, and check that it shows CLEAR, with gray KNOWN rows at most.
  • ☐ If something still alerts, identify it first. Recalibrate only for a signal that you've established belongs to the site's background, or mute that one signal for an hour. An unexplained alert might be a signal you want to detect.

While the unit runs

  • ☐ Don't calibrate while an aircraft or a remote control of the day is on the air. The unit would learn a video transmitter, a control link, and a Wi-Fi aircraft.
  • ☐ Read the icons as well as the level. A red controller alone is a remote control that's switched on. A red camera alone is a video transmitter. Neither one shows that something is flying.
  • ☐ Follow a signal only when you mean to show following. Following pauses the survey of 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz.
  • ☐ Tap the left side of Home to silence one alert until something new arrives. This doesn't mute anything.
  • ☐ Restart the master only when you need to. A restart forgets every mute and starts a new log file. It keeps known signals and settings.
  • ☐ Check the light at the lower left from time to time. Yellow or red means that a sensor isn't listening.

After the event

  • ☐ Switch the unit off, and unplug the battery.
  • ☐ Take the card. Its logs hold what the unit listed and when.

Troubleshooting

What you see Likely cause What to do
The screen is dim. The screen dims after 30 s without a touch. Touch the screen. It never turns fully dark on its own.
The screen is dark and stays dark. The master is off, the battery is empty, or brightness is 0. Hold the power button for about 1 s. Connect USB to the master's own port. Set brightness over USB.
SCANNING is yellow with a warning sign, and a port shows DOWN with NO SENSOR. The sensor on that port doesn't respond. It isn't powered, is asleep, has stopped responding, is being flashed, or a wire is loose. Disconnect the battery and every USB cable, wait a few seconds, and then reconnect them. The power button alone doesn't restart a sensor.
NO RADIO in red. Neither sensor responds. Do the same as for a missing sensor.
A port shows FAULT in red. The sensor reports that its radio failed, or it doesn't accept the master's commands. Wait a minute. The master restarts a sensor with a failed radio automatically, up to three times. If the port stays red, disconnect all power as for a missing sensor.
A port stays yellow. The sensor responds but doesn't start listening. On the Ports screen, read RADIO and SETTINGS. A refused setting suggests a sensor build that doesn't match this master. Note both versions.
RECORDS LOST keeps climbing. A poor connection between that sensor and the master. Check the wires of that port. See UART wires in the wiring guide.
No sound at an alert. The alert was silenced by a tap, volume is 0, or the signal is a notice, a known signal, or muted. Look for SOUND OFF on the left. Check the row: a yellow tag, KNOWN, and MUTE are silent. Check volume over USB.
The sound becomes quiet. After 6 s, an alert continues at about a third of the volume. Nothing. Something new restarts the sound at full volume.
An alert with nothing flying. A signal of the place, very often a fixed wireless camera (video) or a remote control that's switched on (control). The icons and the row show which kind. Identify the signal. If it belongs to the place, calibrate with your own equipment off, or tap the row and mute it for a time.
A short CTRL alert, a few seconds long, with no remote control switched on. Something nearby briefly sends with the timing of a control link. On one unit this happened about three times in a day, from an unknown source. If it keeps happening, set the RF sensor to low. A control link must then last 15 s before it alerts.
A SUB row comes and goes with nothing flying. Another user of the 868 MHz band. It's a notice unless it becomes control. Calibrate, or mute ALL SUB-GHZ for the hour.
A HOP row that never becomes CTRL. Hopping energy on 2.4 GHz that the sensor can't verify as a control link, such as Bluetooth or a control system whose packet rate the sensor doesn't check. It's a silent notice. Nothing to do.
A WIFI row with a drone maker's name. A network or device that looks like drone equipment but might be anything, such as a phone's hotspot with that kind of name. It's a notice. It becomes a LINK and an alert after data flows both ways.
A WAIR row, and none of your aircraft is on. A Wi-Fi network that names itself as an aircraft's. Someone's aircraft is switched on nearby, or a network uses that kind of name. If it belongs to the place, calibrate. The unit then knows it.
A gray KNOWN row turned red. The known signal returned more than 10 dB stronger than when it was learned. Treat it as an alert. If it's the same harmless source, now closer, calibrate again. The unit then knows it up to the new level.
Your own aircraft raises no alert. It was on during a calibration, it's muted, or the unit doesn't detect its kind of link. Look for a KNOWN or MUTE row with its name, and forget it or unmute it. Then compare it with what the unit can detect.
The aircraft icon isn't red, although something is flying. The unit heard only the remote control (the controller is red) or only the video (the camera is red). The icons show what the unit heard. The whole aircraft turns red when the unit hears both.
FOLLOW isn't lit. The signal is of a kind that can't be followed, or the RF sensor was built without the seeker. See Follow one signal.
The time is wrong, or ends in UTC. The master takes its time from the phone. The phone's clock is wrong, or the program that set the time didn't send its time zone. Correct the phone's clock and connect again.
Dashes in place of the time. Nobody has set the clock since the master started. The master doesn't keep the time while it's off. Connect the phone.
CARD NOT USABLE. The card isn't FAT16 or FAT32. Cards larger than 32 GB are sold in another format. Format the card as FAT32 on a computer.
CARD FULL. Less than 8 MB of space is left. Remove old logs on a computer.
NO CARD with a card in the slot. The card doesn't respond. The master checks again once a minute. Switch the unit off, remove the card and insert it again, and then switch the unit on.
A one-minute gap in a log, followed by a new file. A write to the card failed, and the master unmounted the card for a minute. Nothing can be done on the unit. Keep the files together, because the next file continues the log.
The battery icon always shows full. A battery that's charging reads higher than its charge. Unplug the USB cable to read the charge.
The unit restarts over and over. It's powered from a sensor's USB port alone. Connect the battery, or connect USB to the master's own port.

Each sensor board has a small light whose pattern shows what that sensor sees. The patterns are in the sensors' user guides: Remote ID sensor and RF sensor. Not every pattern has been checked by eye.
