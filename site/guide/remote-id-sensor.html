The Remote ID sensor: a user's page

This page is for someone who has the finished unit in hand. The unit as a whole is described in the unit's guide; this page covers one of its two sensors.

  • Describes: detector 0.7.3, read from the code on 15 Nov 2026
  • How sure each statement is: "measured" means seen on the one sensor that exists, on a bench, by the rid-sensor agent, on the date given in the linked record. "Read" means taken from the code or from a maker's page. Nothing here was measured with a real aircraft.

1. What it is for

The Remote ID sensor listens for three things:

  • Remote ID: the identity that many aircraft must broadcast by law, over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.
  • Wi-Fi drones: aircraft that are flown from a phone over an ordinary Wi-Fi network.
  • Control links on 868 MHz: the kind of long-range radio control that ExpressLRS uses.

It only listens. It never transmits on any radio.

What it cannot hear

  • Most DJI aircraft, unless they broadcast Remote ID. Their own link between aircraft and controller (OcuSync) is not Wi-Fi, and this sensor has no receiver for it. The unit's RF sensor may see its energy.
  • Older DJI models with "Enhanced Wi-Fi" (Mavic Mini, Mini SE, the Mavic Air of 2018). Their channels are too narrow for an ordinary Wi-Fi receiver (read: vendor hints).
  • A Wi-Fi drone whose network name it does not know, or one that the owner has renamed. The names it knows are listed in vendor hints.
  • Wi-Fi on the 5 GHz channels 52 to 144. It visits channels 1 to 13, 36 to 48 and 149 to 165.
  • Control links that do not use LoRa, for example Crossfire's fast mode, and anything in the 915 MHz band, which the project does not use in Europe.
  • An aircraft that sends nothing, or one that flies a stored route with its radio off.

It also takes turns: it listens on one Wi-Fi channel at a time, then for Bluetooth. A short broadcast can fall into a gap. Hearing nothing does not show that nothing is there.

2. Quickstart

  1. Fit both antennas before power. The 2.4/5.8 GHz antenna goes on the small socket of the XIAO board. The 868 MHz antenna goes on the socket of the Wio-SX1262 board beneath it. Without them the sensor looks alive and hears almost nothing (read: wiring guide).

  2. Switch the unit on. The sensor gets its power from the master and starts with it.

  3. Look at the master's status bar. One port reads RID and turns green within a few seconds. That is this sensor, linked and listening. Which port it is does not matter.

  4. Look at the sensor's yellow light, if you can see the board:

    Light Meaning
    One short flash every 2 s Running, nothing heard
    Even blink, once a second A Wi-Fi name, a link or a DJI element in the last 5 s
    Steady on A Remote ID decoded in the last 5 s
    Fast blink A radio fault

    The pattern is read from the code. Nobody has confirmed it by eye yet.

  5. Show it a Remote ID, if you have a source: an aircraft that broadcasts one, switched on nearby, or a phone app that sends a test Remote ID. The master lists a row tagged RID with the name the broadcast gives. On the bench this took 2 to 3 s for a source on Wi-Fi channel 6 (measured with the project's test transmitter: master's record).

3. What its reports mean

The master shows each of these as a row; the tag is the word in the row's box.

Tag What was heard What it means for you
RID A Remote ID, decoded An aircraft, or its controller, says who and where it is. The name and position are what the broadcast claims; nobody has checked them
DJI A Wi-Fi frame with DJI's own element A DJI device of an older Wi-Fi kind is near. The sensor recognises the element and does not decode it. Never tried over the air
LINK Data flowing both ways on a network with a drone's name or a drone maker's address Something is being flown or operated over Wi-Fi. It can also be a camera, a controller being set up, or a file download from a landed aircraft
WIFI A network name or an address that belongs to a drone maker A hint only: a drone, its controller, or anyone who named a hotspot DJI-something. A notice, not an alert
SUB, "control" LoRa packets of the kind ExpressLRS sends, on the watched 868 MHz channel, in several seconds running A long-range control link is probably on the air. Other LoRa users of the band can raise it for a few seconds
SUB, "bursts" Short transmissions on the 868 MHz channel that are not recognised A notice only. The band is shared with meters, alarms and sensors

The signal strength shown beside a row says how strongly this sensor heard it. It is not a distance.

4. How to

Update its firmware

Today this goes through the sensor's own USB-C socket. You need the three image files of a release (bootloader.bin, partition-table.bin, c5_drone_detector.bin) and Python with esptool and pyserial.

  1. Plug a USB cable from the computer into the sensor's socket, not the master's. Find its port name, for example /dev/cu.usbmodem1101.

  2. Ask the running sensor to enter its loader:

    python3 -c 'import serial,sys; serial.Serial(sys.argv[1],115200).write(b"\n{\"cmd\":\"boot\",\"id\":4000000002,\"mode\":\"download\"}\n")' /dev/cu.usbmodem1101

  3. Wait five seconds, then write the images:

    python3 -m esptool --chip esp32c5 --port /dev/cu.usbmodem1101 --baud 460800 \
  --before no-reset --after watchdog-reset write-flash \
  --flash-mode dio --flash-freq 80m --flash-size 8MB \
  0x2000 bootloader.bin 0x8000 partition-table.bin 0x10000 c5_drone_detector.bin

  4. The sensor restarts by itself. The master's port turns green again, and the master's Ports screen shows the new version.

[!WARNING] Never leave out --before no-reset --after watchdog-reset. The chip on this board is a revision 1.0 ESP32-C5. esptool's usual reset left such a chip hung, with its USB dead, until a full reset (measured on a bench board of the same chip, 13 Nov 2026). For the same reason, do not press the board's buttons to enter the loader, and do not flash it with a tool that resets it by itself.

The same steps through a bench computer are in detector/tools/portal_flash.sh, which is how every flash so far was done. The two commands above were not run in exactly this form from a laptop.

Run it alone on USB

The sensor works without the master. Power it from a computer's USB port and it starts listening after 3 s. It then sends one line of JSON for everything it hears, and a health line every 5 s. The viewer shows these in a browser, on your own computer, and uploads nothing.

Check that the 868 MHz board is seated and answers

With the sensor on USB, send it one line:

python3 -c 'import serial,sys,time; s=serial.Serial(sys.argv[1],115200,timeout=2); s.write(b"\n{\"cmd\":\"sg_probe\",\"id\":4000000003}\n"); time.sleep(1); print(s.read(4000).decode(errors="replace"))' /dev/cu.usbmodem1101

An answer with "present":true means the radio answers. If it is false, the board has lost contact. Pressed on without solder, the radio of the bench sensor never answered; soldered, it has answered ever since (measured, 13 and 14 Nov 2026).

In the unit, the master tells you the same: a sensor with a working 868 MHz radio sends its level on that band every second.

5. When something is wrong

What you see Likely cause What to do
The RID port turns red for a few seconds, then green A radio fault. The sensor switched its receivers off and started them again by itself, 2 s later (measured once, 15 Nov 2026) Nothing. If it happens often, note the time and report it
The RID port stays red The fault did not clear, or the master cannot talk to this build Switch the unit off and on. If it stays, update the sensor's firmware
The port never leaves -- or grey The sensor has no power, or the two wires between it and the master are loose or crossed Check the wiring against the wiring guide
Nothing from 868 MHz is ever listed, and the phone says coverage is reduced The Wio-SX1262 board does not answer, or stopped answering Run the check above. The sensor goes on with Remote ID meanwhile
Rows tagged SUB come and go with no aircraft near Another user of the band nearby Calibrate the unit at that place, as the unit's guide describes. With the master's sub-GHz level at 12 the bench saw one such notice in two hours (measured, 15 Nov 2026: record)
The sensor is dark and silent while the unit charges It sleeps when the master is off and no computer is on its USB. This is meant, to save the cell Switch the unit on. Sleep and wake are built and not yet tried on hardware
The sensor's USB port is dead after a flash The chip is hung in its loader, see the warning above Unplug every source of power, the cell included, then plug in again
A Remote ID source nearby is not listed Its broadcast fell on a channel or a moment the sensor was not listening, or it sends on a 5 GHz channel the sensor visits only every other second Wait 30 s. Broadcasts repeat, and the sensor comes round to each channel about once a second
