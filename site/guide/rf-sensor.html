RF sensor user guide

This guide is for you if you have a finished unit and want to know what its RF sensor does. The RF sensor is the small board that listens for a drone's radio links. The unit's user guide covers the whole unit, its screen, and the app.

Applies to the RF sensor firmware that checks a control link's packet rate, as of 15 November 2026. The section How this is known names the exact build.

What the sensor listens for

The RF sensor listens at 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz for the two radio links that a hobby or FPV drone uses: the control link from the pilot's handset, and the video that the aircraft sends back. The sensor reports what it detects to the master, and the master decides what to show. The sensor doesn't transmit.

Signal What the sensor does What the master shows
Analog video at 5.8 GHz Reports video, with the channel's name, such as F8 or R7 A VID row
Digital video at 5.8 GHz Reports a wide, flat, steady carrier as digital video A VID row
ExpressLRS control at 2.4 GHz, at 50, 100, 150, 250, 333, or 500 packets per second Reports a verified control link and its rate A CTRL row
Other control systems at 2.4 GHz, such as Crossfire, FrSky, or a DJI handset Reports hopping, but never a verified link A HOP notice when the sensor is confident enough, otherwise no row. Hopping alone never raises an alert
Control at 868 or 915 MHz Nothing. This board has no radio for these bands. The Remote ID sensor watches 868 MHz Nothing from this sensor
DJI's own link, OcuSync, at 5.8 GHz Reports digital video A VID row
DJI's own link at 2.4 GHz Nothing. The sensor looks for video at 5.8 GHz only Nothing
Wi-Fi at 2.4 GHz Tracks a wide carrier No row
A busy Wi-Fi network at 5.8 GHz Tracks a wide carrier and marks it as Wi-Fi, not video A WIFI notice. It never raises an alert by itself
Bluetooth Counts it as background so that it isn't mistaken for a control link Nothing
Remote ID, or a drone's Wi-Fi name Nothing. The Remote ID sensor detects these Nothing from this sensor

At 5.8 GHz, the sensor covers about 5650 to 5940 MHz. Two of the 40 FPV channels, E4 and E8, are at the edges and outside this range.

The sensor's range is much shorter than a drone's own receiver. It reports video only when the signal is well above its noise, and control only near the handset. For what the whole unit can detect, see the unit guide's detection section.

Get started

  1. Before you switch the unit on, connect the antenna to the sensor board's antenna connector. Without the antenna, the sensor receives only signals at arm's length.

  2. Switch the unit on. The master starts the sensor. You don't need to set anything on the sensor.

  3. Check the small light on the sensor board:

    Light Meaning
    Off The radio is off. The sensor is asleep, waiting for the master, or in its loader
    A short blink every 2 seconds Listening, nothing detected
    Two blinks a second A control link
    Five blinks a second Video
    On without blinking Video and control together

  4. On the master, tap PORTS. The sensor's port shows RF SENSOR, UP, and the firmware name. For the master's screens, see the master's README.

  5. Optional: Test the sensor with a transmitter. Switch on an ExpressLRS handset a few steps away, or an analog video transmitter on any channel except E4 and E8. Within a few seconds the light changes as the table shows, and the master shows a detection. A handset at its lowest power next to the sensor is verified after 1 to 4 seconds at 250 packets per second and faster, and after up to 12 seconds at 100 packets per second.

What the sensor reports

The master and the app use their own words for these reports. The names in code font are the sensor's.

Report What it means
Verified control (ctl_verified, with a rate) The sensor found bursts that hop across the band and keep an exact rate of 50 to 500 per second, and then found them again. This is the sensor's strongest statement about a control link. It means that something transmits like an ExpressLRS handset. It doesn't say what the device is or where it is
Hopping without a verified link (nb_fhss) Short bursts hop across 2.4 GHz, but the sensor didn't confirm a steady rate. Bluetooth does this constantly. The master shows a HOP notice when the sensor is confident enough, and never raises an alert on hopping alone. Hopping also appears for up to 10 seconds after each start of the sensor
Analog video (video_analog, with a channel name) A steady carrier with the width and shape of an analog FPV transmitter is on that channel. Once the sensor reports this, it's usually right
Digital video (video_digital) A wide, flat carrier is at 5.8 GHz, and it doesn't come and go like Wi-Fi. continuous or tdd shows whether it transmits constantly or in turns
Wi-Fi carrier (video_digital marked as Wi-Fi) A busy Wi-Fi network at 5.8 GHz. The master shows a WIFI notice
Wide carrier (wb_ofdm) Something wide is present that the sensor doesn't call video. It might be a Wi-Fi network, a weak video signal, or one of the receiver's own humps. The master doesn't list it by itself
Faint video The master's term. A signal that was video a moment ago is now only a wide carrier at the same frequency. The master keeps the row for up to a minute and marks it faint. Usually the transmitter is still there and moving away
Video and control together The master shows the alert as CONFIRMED

Caution

A verified control link doesn't prove that a drone is present. In a lab test with no project transmitter on, two sensors a few meters apart both reported a verified link at the same moments. The source was another device in the building that transmits at the same rate. What that device is isn't known.

Follow one signal

When you ask the master to follow a signal, this sensor stops surveying and stays on that signal. It reports the signal's strength five times a second. During that time, the sensor doesn't detect anything new, and the master keeps the rest of the list as it was. The sensor returns to surveying automatically when you leave that screen.

The strength rises as you move closer to the transmitter. It isn't a distance.

Only firmware built with the seeker option can follow a signal. In that firmware, the sensor's hello record lists seek. For the design, see the seeker notes.

Update the firmware

You update the RF sensor through its own USB-C port. The firmware page doesn't include RF sensor firmware.

Warning

Don't let a tool reset this board. The chip on these sensors is a revision 1.0 ESP32-C5. When a tool resets it through the serial port's control lines, the chip can stop in its loader until you remove all power. Keep --before no-reset in the esptool command, don't use esptool's default reset or its USB reset, and don't flash the board with OpenOCD.

You need the three files of a build (bootloader.bin, partitions.bin, and firmware.bin), a USB-C cable, and a computer with esptool and a serial terminal.

  1. Connect the cable to the sensor's USB-C port, not the master's. The unit can stay on.

  2. Close any other program that uses the port.

  3. In a serial terminal, open the sensor's port, type BOOTLOADER, and press Enter. The light turns off. Close the terminal.

    Opening a serial port can change its control lines on some computers and terminals. If the sensor stops responding, remove all power sources, then reconnect them.

  4. Write the files. Replace PORT with your port's name:

    esptool --chip esp32c5 --port PORT --baud 460800 \
  --before no-reset --after watchdog-reset \
  write-flash --flash-mode dio --flash-freq 80m --flash-size 4MB \
  0x2000 bootloader.bin 0x8000 partitions.bin 0x10000 firmware.bin

The sensor restarts automatically, and the master's PORTS screen shows the new firmware name.

If the write fails partway, the sensor stays in its loader, with its light off, until you write it again. Run step 4 again. You don't need to repeat step 3.

Use the sensor alone on USB

Connect the sensor to a computer, with nothing connected to its two link pins. After 3 seconds without a master, the sensor starts listening, and the light shows what it detects.

  • To print the firmware's name and version, type INFO in a serial terminal.
  • To print every report as a line of text, type SLTAP 1. To stop, type SLTAP 0.

On a charger, without a computer, the sensor sleeps after 2 seconds. For what to do, see Troubleshooting.

Settings in a measuring build

A measuring build is made with -DSENSOR_TUNE=1. A normal build doesn't have these settings. They let you change how the control search works while the sensor runs, to test a change before it's built in. You don't need them to use the unit.

  • TUNE? prints the seven numbers in use. TUNE 0 0 0 0 0 0 0 is the build's own behavior.
  • TUNE 8 18 8 18 0 0 1 detects a weaker control link, about 6 dB weaker, and asks for one more confirmation before it reports a verified link.
  • The sensor loses every setting when it restarts. A tuned unit that you switch off and on again works like a normal unit.

Troubleshooting

What you see Likely cause What to do
The sensor's port doesn't come up on the master, and the sensor's light is off No power, a loose link wire, the sensor is asleep, or the sensor is in its loader after a failed update Check the three wires to the master against the wiring guide. Switch the unit off and on. If an update failed, update the firmware again
The port is up and the light blinks every 2 seconds, but the sensor doesn't detect a transmitter next to it The antenna isn't connected, the transmitter is a kind the sensor can't detect, or the video is on E4 or E8 Connect the antenna. Test with an ExpressLRS handset, or an analog transmitter on another channel
The master shows the sensor's radio as error, or restarts the sensor The radio didn't start after a restart. The sensor resets itself up to twice and then reports error. The master then restarts it up to three times Wait a minute. If the error stays, switch the unit off and on. A full loss of power clears it
On USB, a line appears once a second, and the sensor accepts only BOOTLOADER and REBOOT Safe mode. The firmware failed early four times in a row and waits to be replaced. It tries a normal start automatically after 60 seconds Note the number in the line, and then update the firmware with a known good build
A sensor on a charger, alone, can't be reached over USB With no master and no computer connected, the sensor sleeps after 2 seconds, and its USB port is off while it sleeps Disconnect it and connect it to a computer, or switch on the unit's master. Either one wakes it

If a video row on a nearby channel doesn't clear, wait a minute

Known limitations

  • Only the steady light has been checked by eye. The other patterns come from the code.
  • The antenna's effect hasn't been measured.
  • Safe mode hasn't been seen on a board.
  • The sensor hasn't been tested on the unit's battery.
  • No drone has been tested in the air, and nothing on this page was measured outdoors.
  • No range in meters has been measured.
  • Digital video and OcuSync have been tested with a bench signal generator only. No real digital video system has been tested.
  • No control system other than ExpressLRS has been tested.
  • The Wi-Fi marking at 5.8 GHz has been tested with the bench signal generator's Wi-Fi only.
